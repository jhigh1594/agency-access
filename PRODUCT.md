# Product

## Register

product

## Users

AuthHub serves digital marketing agency owners and operators who manage access across many client advertising, analytics, commerce, and marketing platforms. They work in a high-trust, interruption-prone workflow where they need to request access, guide a client through authorization or manual setup, and quickly understand what is complete, incomplete, revoked, or blocked.

The primary product surfaces are the agency dashboard, access-request workflow, and client authorization experience. Marketing pages are important supporting surfaces, but product UI is the default design register.

## Product Purpose

AuthHub replaces fragmented emails, screenshots, passwords, and follow-up messages with one guided client-facing link and a truthful agency-facing view of access status. It exists to make client onboarding faster, safer, and easier to operate across platforms.

Success means an agency can create and send a request with confidence, a client can complete it without specialist knowledge, and both sides can understand the next required action without guesswork.

## Brand Personality

Confident, direct, reassuring.

AuthHub should feel competent without becoming clinical, concise without becoming terse, and calm without hiding important risk or uncertainty. Its voice should help agency operators act decisively while making unfamiliar authorization steps feel manageable to clients.

## Anti-references

AuthHub should not resemble generic polished SaaS assembled from interchangeable cards, gradients, and decorative metrics. It should not feel like a playful consumer app that trivializes security-sensitive work. It should also avoid the intimidating density, jargon, and visual austerity of a developer console.

## Design Principles

1. **Make status truthful.** Distinguish complete, partial, pending, revoked, failed, and unknown states; never smooth uncertainty into false success.
2. **Make the next action obvious.** Each screen should help the user understand what happened, who needs to act, and what to do next.
3. **Earn trust through clarity.** Explain access, permissions, and errors in plain language while preserving the details needed to diagnose problems.
4. **Guide clients without condescension.** Use progressive disclosure and familiar controls so non-specialists can complete sensitive workflows confidently.
5. **Keep the tool subordinate to the work.** Favor consistent, familiar product patterns over decorative novelty; save emphasis for decisions and state changes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across product and supporting marketing surfaces. Support complete keyboard navigation, visible focus states, screen-reader semantics, reduced-motion preferences, sufficient text and control contrast, and status communication that never relies on color alone.

Use plain language for agency operators and clients with varying technical familiarity. Error, loading, empty, and partial-success states must remain understandable at common mobile and desktop sizes and under zoom or text enlargement.
